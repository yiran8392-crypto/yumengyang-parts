P0106 — Manifold absolute pressure circuit range/performance

OBD-II fault code · Air metering & mixture · updated 2026-11-10 · English version is the reference

P0106 is the plausibility version of the MAP code. The sensor is producing a signal; the ECU just does not believe it for the throttle position it is seeing.

What the code actually means

The ECU compares manifold pressure with what it expects from throttle angle and engine speed. A reading that stays high when vacuum should be strong, or drops when it should not, triggers P0106.

In practice this is a vacuum integrity code. The sensor is usually right and the manifold pressure is genuinely wrong, because something is leaking or something is blocked.

What you will notice

  • A hunting or high idle
  • Poor fuel economy with fuel trim drifting
  • Hesitation when accelerating from idle
  • Sometimes a whistle or hiss from the engine bay
  • A boost car that over-boosts or under-boosts

Our order of checks

CauseOur categoryWhy it is on the list
Vacuum leak at a hose, gasket or PCV—The number one cause. The manifold cannot hold vacuum, so the reading is wrong.
Blocked MAP port or hose—Carbon blocks the port and the sensor reads atmospheric pressure all the time.
Failed MAP sensorIntake pressure sensors (MAP)Real, but only after the vacuum system is proven sound.
Sticking throttle bodyThrottle bodiesThe comparison uses throttle angle. A body that does not close cleanly breaks it.

Test before you buy

  1. Watch fuel trim at idle and at 2,500 rpm. High at idle, normal at speed means a vacuum leak.
  2. Spray carb cleaner around the intake gasket and hoses while watching trim and idle.
  3. Pull the MAP hose and check it for blockage and cracks.
  4. Compare the MAP reading at key-on-engine-off with atmospheric pressure.

The expensive mistake

Chasing a vacuum leak with parts instead of a smoke machine. Replacing the MAP sensor, the PCV and the intake gasket in turn costs more than one smoke test and rarely finds the split hose that is actually leaking.

Questions we get

Is P0106 a sensor fault?
Rarely. It is a plausibility fault, and the manifold pressure really is wrong most of the time.
Can a vacuum leak set P0106 and P0171 together?
Yes, that pair is the classic vacuum leak signature.
