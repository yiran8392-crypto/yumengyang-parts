P0336 — Crankshaft position sensor A circuit range/performance

OBD-II fault code · Crank, cam & timing · updated 2026-11-10 · English version is the reference

P0336 is the code that means "the signal is there, but I do not believe it". That is a different problem from P0335, and it is the one where a new sensor most often fails to fix anything.

What the code actually means

The ECU is receiving crankshaft pulses but the pattern is implausible: wrong tooth count, an unexpected gap, or a speed change that the engine could not physically produce. It stores P0336 because the signal exists but cannot be trusted for timing.

That makes the tone ring, the air gap and the wiring the prime suspects, not the sensor electronics. The sensor is usually reporting accurately what it sees.

What you will notice

  • Rough running or a misfire that moves between cylinders
  • Hard starting, sometimes with a backfire
  • An intermittent stall with the engine restarting immediately
  • The light on with the car driving almost normally in mild cases

Our order of checks

CauseOur categoryWhy it is on the list
Cracked, rusty or damaged tone ring—The number one cause of P0336. Missing or deformed teeth make the pattern implausible.
Incorrect air gap—A sensor fitted without its shim, or a worn mount, reads a weak and uneven signal.
Metal debris on the sensor tip—Iron filings from a worn bearing or a recent repair bridge the gap and distort the signal.
Failing crankshaft position sensorCrankshaft position sensorsReal but less common than the ring. Only condemn it after the ring checks out.
Loom chafe causing intermittent loss—A partially broken wire drops pulses irregularly, which the ECU reads as range failure.

Test before you buy

  1. Remove the sensor and look at the ring through the hole. Cracks, rust and missing teeth are visible more often than people expect.
  2. Wipe the sensor tip and check for metal debris before refitting.
  3. Check the air gap against the specification for that engine family — do not assume it is the same as the last car you did.
  4. Scope the signal if you can. A clean square or sine wave rules out the wiring; a ragged one points at the loom.

The expensive mistake

Replacing the sensor because the code says "sensor circuit". P0336 names the circuit, not the part. If the tone ring is cracked, a new sensor changes nothing and the return comes back as "still faulty".

Questions we get

What is the difference between P0335 and P0336?
P0335 means no usable signal. P0336 means a signal that is present but does not make sense. The second one is usually mechanical.
Can a P0336 be intermittent?
Yes, and that is the signature of a partly broken wire or a ring that only distorts when hot.
