P0101 — Mass air flow circuit range/performance

OBD-II fault code · Air metering & mixture · updated 2026-11-10 · English version is the reference

P0101 is the most misdiagnosed code in the intake group. The sensor is reporting a real number; the number just does not match what the engine is doing.

What the code actually means

The ECU compares measured airflow against a model built from throttle position, engine speed and manifold pressure. When the measured value drifts outside the expected window for a set time, it stores P0101.

That makes it a comparison fault. It can be caused by the sensor reading low (contamination), reading high (a leak before the sensor), or by the model being wrong (a sticking throttle, a restricted intake).

What you will notice

  • Hesitation on light throttle, then a surge as the ECU corrects
  • Long-term fuel trim drifting positive or negative
  • Rough idle on some engines, normal running above 2,000 rpm
  • Occasional stall at a stop, especially on turbo cars

Leak between air filter and MAF—Unmetered air skews the reading high. A missing or split housing seal is enough.

Test before you buy

  1. Log MAF g/s and throttle position together. Compare the shape with a known-good car of the same model — not with a number from a manual.
  2. Check fuel trim at idle and at 2,500 rpm. Trim that is bad at idle and good at speed is a vacuum leak, not a MAF.
  3. Inspect the intake path from the filter to the throttle for splits and missing clamps.
  4. Clean the element with MAF cleaner before replacing — it is the cheapest test there is.

The expensive mistake

Replacing a good MAF on a car with a leaking intake boot. P0101 names the circuit but describes a disagreement. Ask for fuel trim numbers at idle and at 2,500 rpm — that single pair tells you whether you are looking at a leak or a sensor.

Questions we get

Is P0101 always the MAF sensor?
No. It is a plausibility fault. Air leaks and restricted filters cause it at least as often as a failing sensor.
Can a dirty MAF cause P0101 without any other code?
Yes, and it is common. The sensor is still working, just reading wrong.
