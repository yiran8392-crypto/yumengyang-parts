P0011 — Camshaft timing over-advanced or system performance (bank 1)

OBD-II fault code · Crank, cam & timing · updated 2026-11-10 · English version is the reference

P0011 is the most common VVT code in the world, and it is nearly always an oil problem wearing the mask of an electrical one. The oil control valve is the part to look at first.

What the code actually means

The ECU commands the intake cam phaser to a target angle and compares it with the actual angle from the cam sensor. When the cam stays more advanced than commanded for longer than the calibration allows, it sets P0011.

Phasers are moved by oil pressure through the oil control valve. So P0011 is an oil circuit fault far more often than a sensor fault — sludge, a clogged screen, low oil pressure or the wrong oil grade all produce it.

What you will notice

  • Rough idle and a stumble just off idle
  • Poor fuel economy and a lazy throttle response
  • A rattle or tick from the front of the head on cold start
  • Sometimes no symptom at all until the light comes on
  • P0011 together with P0014 (exhaust cam) points at oil supply, not at two valves

Sludge in the oil galleries—Long oil change intervals block the phaser feed and the cam cannot follow the command.

Test before you buy

  1. Watch commanded and actual cam angle on live data while revving. If actual never follows, it is the valve or the oil feed.
  2. Remove the oil control valve and inspect the screen. Sludge here is the diagnosis.
  3. Check the oil level and condition — a P0011 on dirty oil is not a parts problem.
  4. Measure the solenoid resistance and compare with the specification before ordering a valve.

The expensive mistake

Replacing the phaser before the valve. The valve is a fraction of the price, it is the part that fails most often, and it takes minutes to clean. If a customer has already been quoted a phaser, ask whether the OCV screen was checked first.

Questions we get

Is P0011 the sensor or the oil valve?
Usually the oil control valve or an oil supply problem. The sensor is rarely the cause.
Can I drive with a P0011?
Yes, but the cam is in the wrong place, so economy and emissions suffer and the phaser wears faster.
