P0420 — Catalyst system efficiency below threshold (bank 1)

OBD-II fault code · Oxygen sensor & emissions · updated 2026-11-10 · English version is the reference

P0420 is the most expensive misdiagnosis in this list. The catalyst is usually the victim; the cause is upstream, and it is often a part we sell for a fraction of the price.

What the code actually means

The ECU compares the upstream and downstream oxygen sensor signals. A working catalyst stores and releases oxygen, so the downstream signal should be much steadier than the upstream one. When the two signals look too similar, the ECU concludes the catalyst has lost its storage capacity and sets P0420.

It never says the catalyst is broken — it says the catalyst is not performing. A lazy upstream sensor, a small exhaust leak or a misfire can all produce the same comparison result. Diagnose the cause before quoting a catalyst.

What you will notice

  • Engine light with no driveability change at all — the most common presentation
  • A sulphur or rotten-egg smell after a hard drive
  • Slightly worse fuel economy
  • Failing an emissions test
  • Sometimes a rattle from the exhaust when the catalyst substrate breaks up

Our order of checks

CauseOur categoryWhy it is on the list
Lazy upstream oxygen sensorOxygen sensorsThe comparison is only as good as the sensors. A slow upstream sensor mimics a dead catalyst.
Misfires or rich running over timeIgnition coils, Fuel injectorsUnburnt fuel overheats the substrate and washes the washcoat off. This is how catalysts actually die.
Oil or coolant burning—Burns or poisons the catalyst. A new one will die the same way if the engine is not fixed.
Exhaust leak before the downstream sensor—Air entering the exhaust makes the downstream signal look active, which reads as a failed catalyst.
Genuinely failed catalyst—Real, but it should be the last conclusion, not the first.

Test before you buy

  1. Check for stored misfire and mixture codes. Fix those first — a catalyst will not survive them.
  2. Compare upstream and downstream switching on live data. Downstream should be much flatter.
  3. Look for exhaust leaks before the downstream sensor, and for a rattle inside the can.
  4. Measure oil consumption and check for coolant loss before fitting a new catalyst.

The expensive mistake

Selling a catalyst on a car that has had a misfire for months. The new catalyst dies in weeks and the customer blames the part. Fix the misfire, then the catalyst. Ask for the code history before quoting — that single question protects both of you.

Questions we get

Do I have to replace the catalyst for P0420?
No, and usually you should not be the first thing you try. Check the sensors, misfires and exhaust leaks first.
Can I drive with a P0420?
Yes. It is an emissions fault, not a safety fault. But ignoring the cause will eventually cost a catalyst as well.
How long does a catalyst last?
150,000 km and more if the engine is healthy. A catalyst that fails early is a symptom.
