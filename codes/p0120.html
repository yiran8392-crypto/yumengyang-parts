P0120 — Throttle position sensor A circuit malfunction

OBD-II fault code · Temperature, idle & throttle · updated 2026-11-10 · English version is the reference

P0120 is a limp-mode code. The ECU has lost the throttle position signal, so it no longer knows how far open the throttle is — and it will limit power rather than guess.

What the code actually means

The throttle position sensor reports the angle of the throttle plate. The ECU uses it for fuelling, for the automatic gearbox shift points and for its own plausibility checks. An open or shorted circuit sets P0120.

On drive-by-wire cars the relationship between the pedal and the throttle is electronic, so a TPS fault can also produce a limp home mode with a very low rev limit — which customers often describe as "the car will not go above 2,000 rpm".

What you will notice

  • Limp mode with a low rev limit or reduced power
  • An idle that hangs high or a car that will not idle at all
  • Hesitation and a dead spot just off idle
  • Erratic or harsh gear changes on automatics
  • Sometimes a stall when coming to a stop

Test before you buy

  1. Watch the TPS voltage on live data and sweep the throttle slowly. A dead spot or a jump means the track is worn.
  2. Check the connector and the supply voltage.
  3. Clean the throttle body before replacing anything — carbon causes a surprising share of these.
  4. On drive-by-wire cars, confirm whether the sensor is separate or part of the body before quoting.

The expensive mistake

Selling a sensor for a throttle body that needs cleaning and a relearn. Modern throttles need an adaptation after cleaning, and without it the engine idles badly and the customer assumes the part was wrong.

Questions we get

Is P0120 the sensor or the throttle body?
It depends on the car. Many modern engines integrate the sensor into the body, so the whole unit is replaced.
Can I drive with a P0120?
In limp mode, slowly. It is not a safety failure but the car will be unpleasant and possibly unable to accelerate normally.
