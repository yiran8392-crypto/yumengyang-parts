P0115 — Engine coolant temperature circuit malfunction

OBD-II fault code · Temperature, idle & throttle · updated 2026-11-10 · English version is the reference

P0115 is a boring code with expensive consequences. Without a coolant temperature reading the ECU assumes a warm engine, so it under-fuels from cold and misjudges everything downstream.

What the code actually means

The coolant temperature sensor is a thermistor: resistance falls as temperature rises. The ECU reads the voltage across it and converts it to temperature. An open, shorted or out-of-range circuit sets P0115.

When the signal is lost the ECU substitutes a default value, usually around 80 °C. That default is why the car still runs — and why the cold-start behaviour is wrong.

What you will notice

  • Hard starting when cold, or a fast idle that never drops
  • Rich running and a strong fuel smell on cold start
  • The temperature gauge dead or jumping
  • Radiator fans running when they should not, or not running when they should
  • Higher fuel consumption and worse emissions

Our order of checks

CauseOur categoryWhy it is on the list
Failed coolant temperature sensorTemperature sensorsThe thermistor drifts or opens. Cheap and easy to prove with a meter.
Corroded connector—The sensor sits in a wet, hot place. Pins corrode and the reading drifts.
Damaged wiring—Rodent damage and chafe are common on the sensor loom.
Low coolant or airlock—The sensor must be in coolant to read correctly. An air pocket gives a false cold reading.
Wrong sensor fittedTemperature sensorsThere are often two sensors — one for the gauge and one for the ECU. Do not mix them up.

Test before you buy

  1. Read coolant temperature on live data from a cold start and compare with the gauge and with reality.
  2. Measure sensor resistance and compare with the specification at two temperatures.
  3. Check the connector for corrosion and the wiring for continuity.
  4. Check the coolant level and bleed the system before condemning the sensor — an airlock reads exactly like a fault.

The expensive mistake

Fitting the sensor for the gauge instead of the one for the ECU. Many engines have two, with different resistance curves and sometimes identical connectors. Confirm by resistance value, not by appearance.

Questions we get

Can a bad coolant sensor cause a no-start?
Yes, on cold mornings. The ECU fuels for a warm engine and the mixture is too lean to fire.
How many coolant sensors does an engine have?
Often two: one for the ECU and one for the gauge. They are not always interchangeable.
