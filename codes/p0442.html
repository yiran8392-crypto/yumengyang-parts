P0442 — Evaporative emission system leak detected (small leak)

OBD-II fault code · Oxygen sensor & emissions · updated 2026-11-10 · English version is the reference

P0442 is the small-leak code in the evaporative system. It is usually not a part at all — it is a seal, a cap, or a hose that has gone hard with age.

What the code actually means

The EVAP system seals the fuel tank and vents vapour through a canister into the intake. The ECU pulls a slight vacuum on the sealed system and watches how fast it decays. A decay faster than a calibrated threshold but slower than a large leak sets P0442.

The test only runs under specific conditions — usually a cold start with the tank between a quarter and three quarters full. That is why P0442 can take days to reappear after being cleared.

What you will notice

  • Engine light with no driveability symptom at all
  • A fuel smell around the filler neck or the engine bay
  • The code returning days after being cleared
  • Sometimes a hiss when opening the fuel cap
  • Hard starting after refuelling on some cars

Our order of checks

CauseOur categoryWhy it is on the list
Fuel cap seal—The most common cause by a wide margin. Check the seal and the seating surface first.
Perished or cracked EVAP hose—Rubber hoses harden and split, especially in hot climates.
Sticking canister purge valveCanister purge valvesA valve that does not seal lets vapour escape and can also make the system fail its own test.
Leaking canister or cracked tank vent—Plastic cracks with age and vibration.
Filler neck corrosion—Common on cars from coastal or humid regions.

Test before you buy

  1. Inspect the fuel cap seal for cracks and the filler neck for rust.
  2. Look at every EVAP hose under the bonnet and above the tank for hardening and splits.
  3. Smoke test the system — it finds small leaks faster than any other method.
  4. Test the purge valve for sealing and for correct operation with a scan tool.

The expensive mistake

Selling a purge valve for a leak that is a fuel cap. Ask the customer to check the cap seal and click it properly before you quote anything. This code resolves without parts more often than any other on this list.

Questions we get

Can a loose fuel cap cause P0442?
Yes, it is the number one cause. A cap that is not clicked or has a hardened seal lets the vacuum decay.
Why does the code take days to come back?
The self-test only runs under specific conditions — cold start, partial tank. It can take several drive cycles.
Can I drive with a P0442?
Yes. It is an emissions fault and does not affect how the car drives.
