Throttle body cleaning, P0507 and the electronic throttle relearn

Published 2026-11-20 · 6 min read · English version is the reference

On a cable-operated throttle a dirty body causes a sticky pedal. On a drive-by-wire body it causes a fault code, a 1,500 rpm idle and sometimes a limp home — because the control unit has learned the carbon as part of the closed position. This guide covers the codes, the safe cleaning sequence and the relearn.

Cable or drive-by-wire

Cable bodies are mechanical: a cable pulls the plate, an idle screw sets the base opening, and there is usually a separate idle control valve. Drive-by-wire bodies have a motor, two redundant position sensors and no cable. The control unit continuously learns where "closed" is; carbon on the bore moves that learned position, which is why a freshly cleaned body idles high until it relearns.

Cleaning an electronic body without creating a fault

  1. Key off, battery negative disconnected, then wait a minute — some bodies are driven to a park position at shutdown.
  2. Remove the body from the manifold rather than spraying into it on the car, so the solvent and the carbon do not go into the engine.
  3. Use throttle-body cleaner and a soft cloth. Never push the plate open by hand against the motor, and never scrape the bore.
  4. Do not soak the motor or the electronics side; keep the solvent on the bore and the plate.
  5. Refit with a new gasket, reconnect the battery, then perform the relearn.

The relearn

Three routes, depending on the platform: a scan-tool basic setting (VAG calls it throttle adaptation, channel 060 on older cars), a key-on / key-off cycle repeated a few times, or a drive cycle that lets the control unit re-learn the closed position at idle. If the idle stays high after the relearn, look for a vacuum leak — a split intake boot produces exactly the same P0507.

If the body will not complete a relearn, stop. Forcing adaptation on a worn body writes bad values and the car comes back. Replace it.

What decides the part number

  • Bore size. The same engine family uses different bodies by market and by gearbox.
  • Motor and sensor integration. Some bodies carry the TPS, some do not.
  • Coolant or heater pipes. Cold-climate bodies have coolant passages; the bare version does not.
  • Connector and pin count. Six-pin and eight-pin housings look alike from outside.
  • Programming. A few platforms need the body coded to the car; mention it in the quote so the workshop plans for it.
