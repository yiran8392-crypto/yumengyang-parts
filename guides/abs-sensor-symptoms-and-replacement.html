ABS sensor symptoms, fault codes and part matching

Published 2026-11-02 · 7 min read · English version is the reference

Most "ABS sensor" jobs start with a warning light and end with a wrong-side part. This guide covers the symptoms, the scan data, and the four details that decide the part number.

What the sensor does, and the two types

An ABS wheel-speed sensor reports how fast each wheel is turning. The control unit compares the four signals and releases brake pressure on a wheel that is locking. When one signal drops out, ABS switches off and the light stays on — the brakes still work, but without anti-lock assistance and usually without traction control or stability control.

Symptoms you will actually notice

  • ABS warning lamp on after start-up, sometimes only after the first few metres of driving.
  • Traction control or ESP lamp on at the same time — one dead sensor usually disables both.
  • ABS pulsing or a grinding pedal at low speed when no wheel is slipping, caused by a false signal.
  • Speedometer needle dropping to zero or jumping, on cars that take the speed signal from a wheel sensor.
  • Cruise control refusing to engage, and on some models a gearbox that holds gears longer.
  • Intermittent faults that appear in rain, after a bump, or when the steering is on full lock — classic chafed-cable behaviour.

What the scan tool usually shows

Read the codes from the ABS module, not from the engine module. The table below shows the families you will meet most often — always confirm the exact meaning in the manufacturer's code list, because the same number means different things on different platforms.

VW / Audi / Skoda / Seat00283, 00285, 00287, 00290Wheel-speed sensor, front left / front right / rear right / rear left — the order is fixed and worth memorising.

Live data is more useful than the code. Drive slowly and watch the four wheel-speed values: a sensor that reads 0 km/h while the others read 12 km/h has told you everything.

Test before you replace

  1. Read and record the codes, then clear them and reproduce the fault — a code that returns immediately is a hard fault.
  2. Inspect the harness at the hub: look for cracks where the cable flexes on steering movement, and for corrosion inside the connector.
  3. On a passive sensor, measure resistance across the two pins and compare with the workshop figure; then spin the wheel by hand and watch for AC voltage.
  4. On an active sensor, check the supply with the ignition on, then watch the signal on a scope or in live data while turning the wheel.
  5. Inspect the tone ring or magnetic encoder on the hub — a cracked or rusty ring produces the same symptoms as a dead sensor and costs less to fix.

The four details that decide the part number

  • Corner. Front left, front right, rear left, rear right are four different numbers on most platforms, even when the sensors look identical. Right-hand-drive cars mirror them.
  • Cable length and grommet. The same sensor with a longer cable is a different OE number, and the short one will not reach the body clip.
  • Connector keyway and pin count. Two-pin and three-pin housings are not interchangeable, and some families use the same shell with a different key.
  • Driven axle. An AWD version may use a different rear sensor or a different mounting bracket from the FWD version of the same model.

Why "same model, same year" still fails

Facelifts, brake packages and market-specific assembly plants change sensors on cars that look identical. A 2010 Golf built in Wolfsburg and one built in China can carry different front sensor numbers. The VIN helps you place the car; only the OE number confirms the part — see the VIN guide for how to read the first eleven characters.

Our ABS sensor catalogue lists every model by OE number and cross-reference. Typical entries: 4E0927804 for the front-right position on Audi A4 to A8, and WHT003857 / 7H0927803 for the front-left position on Golf Mk6, A3 and TT.

Before you order

  • Photograph the old sensor, the connector and the mounting point — one picture answers three questions.
  • Note the corner and the cable length, not just the model name.
  • Check whether the fault is the sensor, the harness or the tone ring; two out of three do not need a new sensor.
  • Send the OE number and quantity on WhatsApp for price, MOQ and packing.
