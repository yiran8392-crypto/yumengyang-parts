No-start diagnosis for crankshaft and camshaft position sensors

Published 2026-11-20 · 6 min read · English version is the reference

Two sensors decide whether the engine runs at all: the crankshaft sensor tells the control unit where the crank is, the camshaft sensor where the cam is. When the crank signal disappears there is no spark and no injection — often with no fault code stored, because the control unit never sees a signal to complain about.

What each one does

  • Crankshaft sensor: engine speed and crank position. Lose it and the engine stops or never starts — this is the sensor behind most "cranks but will not fire" cases.
  • Camshaft sensor: which stroke each cylinder is on. Lose it and many engines still run, in batch or limp mode, with a long crank and a P0340.

Prove it in ten minutes

  1. Watch engine speed in live data while cranking. A steady 0 rpm with the starter turning means the control unit is not seeing the crank signal at all.
  2. Check the supply and ground at the connector with the key on — most Hall sensors need a solid 5 V or 12 V feed.
  3. For an inductive sensor, measure resistance and compare with the workshop figure, then crank and look for AC volts on a meter or a scope.
  4. Inspect the sensor tip for metal debris and the reluctor wheel for a chipped tooth — a chipped tooth gives a signal that is present but wrong.
  5. If the fault only appears hot, test again with the engine at temperature. Heat-fail sensors are the reason a car restarts fine twenty minutes later.

What decides the part number

  • Sensor type. An inductive and a Hall sensor for the same engine are not interchangeable, and the connectors differ.
  • Cable length and bracket. The same sensor is sold with different lengths and mounting clips.
  • Target wheel spacing. Air gap is usually under 1.5 mm; a thicker shim or a wrong body produces a P0336 on a brand-new part.
  • Position. Crank, cam inlet, cam exhaust — three different numbers on one engine.

Our catalogues list both families by OE number: crankshaft position sensors and camshaft position sensors. Typical entries: 39180-22600 for Hyundai and Kia 1.4 VVT, and A11-3611011 for the Chery and Geely camshaft range.

Questions buyers ask

Can a crank sensor fail without setting a code?

Yes. A sensor that only fails when hot, or that produces a weak signal, often leaves no code because the control unit never sees a clean failure. Live data and a scope find it.

How do I tell a Hall sensor from an inductive one?

Count the wires: 2 wires is inductive, 3 is Hall or magneto-resistive. An inductive sensor produces its own AC signal; the others need a supply.

Do you supply the connector or the pigtail?

We can quote connectors and repair pigtails on request — they are not on the site. Send a photo of the connector face.

What is the minimum order for these sensors?

10 pcs for most references. Cam and crank sensors of different numbers can share one order.
