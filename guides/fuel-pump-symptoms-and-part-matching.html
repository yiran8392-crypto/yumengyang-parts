Fuel pump symptoms and how to match a pump, hanger or assembly

Published 2026-11-20 · 7 min read · English version is the reference

Roughly half of the jobs quoted as "fuel pump" turn out to be a relay, a clogged filter or a pressure sensor. This guide separates the diagnosis from the purchase, then covers what actually decides the part number: pump only, hanger only, or the complete module.

What the pump actually does

Almost every petrol car built since the 1990s uses an electric pump inside the tank. It pushes fuel forward at low pressure — typically 3 to 4 bar on port injection — and the pressure regulator or the returnless system keeps it there. On a direct-injection engine the in-tank pump only feeds a mechanical high-pressure pump on the engine, so a weak low-pressure pump shows up as a high-pressure fault code, not as a missing one.

Because the pump is cooled and lubricated by the fuel around it, the two things that kill it are running the tank near empty and a blocked filter. Both are cheaper to prevent than the pump is to replace.

Symptoms, from early to terminal

  • Longer cranking before the engine fires, worst after the car has stood overnight.
  • Loss of power under load — a hill or a full-throttle overtake — that disappears when you lift off.
  • Hesitation or a single lean misfire at high rpm, often with no fault code at all.
  • Whining or buzzing from under the rear seat that changes pitch with load; a healthy pump is audible at key-on but quiet once running.
  • Sudden no-start with the engine cranking normally, sometimes restored by a sharp knock on the tank — the classic worn-brush symptom.
  • Intermittent stall in hot weather or after a long drive, then a normal restart after twenty minutes: a pump that is failing hot.

What the codes tell you — and what they do not

CodeReads asWhat it usually means
P0230Fuel pump primary circuit malfunctionThe control side: relay, fuse, wiring or the pump driver in the control unit. Rarely the pump itself.
P0231 / P0232Fuel pump secondary circuit low / highThe monitored feed or ground side. Check voltage at the pump connector under load.
P0087Fuel rail / system pressure too lowA real pressure shortfall: pump, filter, regulator or a leaking injector. Also the code you get from a GDI high-pressure pump.
P0089Fuel pressure regulator performanceThe regulator or its control, not necessarily the pump.
P0627Fuel pump A control circuit openThe module is not seeing the pump circuit at all — check the connector before ordering.

No code is common. A pump that is merely tired stays inside the monitoring window and simply under-delivers. Pressure and volume testing finds it; a code reader does not.

Test before you buy

  1. Measure rail pressure at key-on and again with the engine running under load; compare with the workshop figure for that engine.
  2. Check the pump's current draw with a clamp meter. A pump pulling well above spec is worn; one pulling almost nothing is dead or unpowered.
  3. Listen and feel the relay, then swap it with an identical one from the fuse box — a two-minute test that saves a tank removal.
  4. Measure voltage at the pump connector while cranking. Anything below battery voltage minus a volt points at wiring or the module, not the pump.
  5. Replace the fuel filter before condemning the pump if the car has covered high mileage on the original filter.

A pump that runs but delivers nothing is usually a split hose inside the tank or a failed in-tank regulator — the pump itself is fine. Look inside the module before ordering.

Pump, hanger or complete assembly?

The same car can be sold three ways, and the price difference is large. Knowing which one you are quoting stops the classic argument at the counter.

What you buyWhen it makes senseWatch out for
Bare pump (motor only)The hanger, sender and regulator are healthy and only the motor is tired.You must transfer the strainer, the hose and often the connector — and match the flow rating exactly.
Pump + hangerThe plastic housing or the sender is cracked, or the original is not serviceable.Sender resistance and tank depth must match, or the gauge will read wrong.
Complete moduleFastest and safest for a workshop: everything new, nothing to transfer.Higher price and more variants — the same model may use two different modules by market.

The details that decide the part number

  • Flow and pressure. Two pumps with the same body can be rated differently; fitting the smaller one leans the engine out at high load.
  • Return or returnless. A returnless system has the regulator in the module. Mixing the two gives a permanent pressure fault.
  • Sender resistance. Empty and full values differ between markets; the wrong sender means a gauge that reads backwards.
  • Tank depth and module height. A module that is too short leaves fuel behind, one that is too long fouls the tank floor.
  • Connector and locking ring. Pin count and keyway vary even within one model year.
  • Pump petrol or flex-fuel. Ethanol-resistant seals are a different part number.

Finding it in our catalogue

Our fuel pump catalogue lists every pump by OE number with cross-references and fitment. Typical entries: 1GD919051E for the Jetta and Bora family, and 180919051B for Bora, Jetta and the new Santana / Lavida range.

  • Send the OE number from the old pump, plus a photo of the module top — the connector and the locking ring identify it faster than the model name.
  • If the number is unreadable, send the VIN and a photo of the tank opening.
  • State whether you need the bare pump or the complete module; the price is not comparable.
  • Ask for the packing quantity per carton — pumps ship in foam trays, and that affects the freight calculation.
